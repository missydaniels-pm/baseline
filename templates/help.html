What is Baseline?

Baseline is a tool for people managing chronic health conditions who want real data on what is and isn't working.

Most health trackers just log data. Baseline helps you run structured experiments — introduce one change at a time, track the results, and build an evidence base for your own health decisions. Think of it as giving you the tools to be a scientist of your own health.

Getting Started

Set up what you're tracking

When you first create your account, you'll go through a short setup where you define the 1–3 things you most want to track. Keep it focused — you can always add more later, but starting with what matters most gives you the clearest signal.

Examples: Pain, Fatigue, Cognitive fog, Energy, Mood, Nausea, Mobility.

Establish your baseline

Before you start tracking, Baseline asks you to estimate where you are right now. How many bad days have you had in the last 4 weeks? On your worst days, how would you rate each thing you're tracking?

This gives you a starting point to measure against. It doesn't need to be perfect — a rough honest estimate is enough.

Enable AI logging

Baseline can use AI to make logging as easy as sending a text message. Instead of filling out forms, you describe your day in plain language and Baseline figures out what to record.

This is optional. If you prefer the structured forms, skip it and turn it on later in Settings. If you enable it, your messages are processed by Anthropic's API.

The Daily Check-in

How it works

If you've enabled AI logging, the Daily Check-in is your main way to log how you're doing. Instead of filling out forms, you type naturally — describe your day the way you'd tell a friend. Baseline's AI reads your message, extracts the relevant health data, and creates the right records automatically. It then confirms exactly what it logged so you can verify or correct anything.

Getting to your check-in

Tap Check-in in the sidebar to open the check-in page. If you've chatted before, you'll see your recent conversation history. If it's your first time, you'll see an empty chat ready for your first message.

What to include

You don't need to follow a specific format. Just mention whatever feels relevant:

  • What you're experiencing and how severe it is (a rough number out of 10 helps)
  • Any medications or protocols you took — or missed
  • When things started and how long they lasted
  • How much it affected your ability to function
  • Whether you used an intervention and if it helped
  • Any context — triggers, sleep, stress, weather, whatever seems connected

You don't need all of this every time. A quick "good day, nothing to report" is a perfectly valid check-in.

Example messages

Here are some examples of what you might say:

  • "Pretty rough today, headache around a 7, fatigue at 5, took my Sumatriptan around noon and felt better by 3pm"
  • "Good day, did all my protocols, no symptoms worth noting"
  • "Forgot my magnesium this morning, slight headache in the afternoon around a 3, managed to work through it"
  • "Woke up at a 6, been building all morning, now at an 8. Can't work. Haven't taken anything yet"
  • "Actually yesterday's headache was more like a 5, not a 7 — I think I overestimated"
  • "Took my riboflavin and CoQ10 today. No episodes. Energy was decent, maybe a 4"
  • "Bad flare since 2am, nausea at 8, completely out of commission. Used Zofran and ice pack, slightly better after 2 hours"
  • "Same as yesterday basically — low-grade headache around a 3, functioning fine"

What gets logged automatically

Based on your message, Baseline will create the appropriate records:

  • Episodes — if you describe a significant health event, an episode is created with onset time, duration, and functional impairment
  • Severity scores — ratings are attached to the episode for each thing you're tracking
  • Protocol compliance — if you mention taking or missing a medication/supplement, a compliance record is logged for that protocol. Misses count too — "I missed my magnesium because I ran out" records the miss with your reason. If you already confirmed today on the dashboard, telling the check-in something different updates it (your most recent word wins).
  • Intervention use — if you used one or more interventions, each is recorded separately on the episode with its own effectiveness rating and time to relief

After processing, the AI confirms exactly what it logged. Review the confirmation — if everything looks right, you're done.

Correcting mistakes

If the AI gets something wrong, just say so in your next message. It will update the record:

  • "Actually that headache was a 5, not a 7"
  • "I did take my magnesium today, I just forgot to mention it"
  • "That episode started yesterday evening, not today"

Check-in vs. manual logging

The check-in and the manual episode form both create the same data — episodes, symptom scores, and compliance records. Use whichever feels easier in the moment:

  • Check-in is fastest for quick daily updates and when you want to describe things in your own words
  • Manual logging (via Log Episode) is useful when you want precise control over every field, or if you haven't enabled AI logging

You can mix and match — there's no wrong approach.

Privacy and AI logging

When you use the Daily Check-in, your messages are sent to Anthropic's API for processing. Anthropic does not use your data to train models. You can read their full policy at anthropic.com/privacy.

Baseline's own privacy policy is available at Privacy Policy.

AI logging is entirely optional. You can enable or disable it at any time in Settings. If you turn it off, you can still log everything manually through the episode and protocol forms.

Logging Episodes

An episode is any significant health event worth recording — a bad day, a flare, a symptom spike. You can log episodes through the Daily Check-in or through the structured form in the Episodes section.

For each episode you can record:

  • When it started and how long it lasted
  • Severity for each thing you're tracking
  • How much it affected your ability to function
  • What interventions you used (if any) and how well each one worked — you can log multiple interventions per episode
  • Possible triggers — tap any of the suggested triggers that might have contributed, or type your own with "Add your own." Your custom triggers are saved and offered again next time. This is optional, and you can pick as many as apply.
  • Notes, context, or anything else worth remembering

Protocols

Preventative protocols

These are the ongoing interventions you're using to manage your condition — medications, supplements, lifestyle changes, dietary protocols. Add them in the Protocols section with a start date and notes. There's also an optional "Why I'm doing this" field — if you fill it in, Baseline uses your own words to keep you connected to your goal on days that don't go to plan.

Today's Protocols — daily logging on your dashboard

Your dashboard opens with a Today's Protocols card listing each active protocol, each with a Complete and a Not Today button. Nothing is saved until you act. You have two ways to log:

  • Did everything? If you haven't marked anything yet, the button reads "Confirm all as complete" — one tap logs every protocol as done. Most days that's all you need.
  • Only did some of them? Tap Complete on the ones you finished (and Not Today on any you're skipping — you can add a quick optional note). As soon as you mark anything, the button becomes Save. Saving logs exactly what you marked and leaves the rest unmarked — so you can log your morning doses now and come back tonight for the rest.
  • Changed your mind on a button? Tap Complete or Not Today a second time to clear it back to unmarked. If you clear everything, the button returns to "Confirm all as complete".
  • Already saved something and want to undo it? Tap that protocol's button to clear it, then Save — the entry is removed and the protocol goes back to unmarked.

A "Not Today" isn't a failure — logging it honestly is exactly what makes your experiments trustworthy.

  • The 7-day dots above the list show your last week: green means every protocol that day is logged as complete, amber means you logged the day but it isn't all complete (some marked Not Today, or some still left blank), and a hollow dot means nothing was recorded.
  • Tap a hollow dot to fill in a past day — only log what you actually remember, and use "Not sure — leave blank" freely. A blank day is better data than a guess. Clearing a past entry and saving removes it.
  • Changed your mind? After confirming, tap "change something" to edit today.

Each protocol also has a detail page where you can log or correct a single day and see a full history timeline. The timeline shows every taken and missed entry alongside status changes — when you started, paused, stopped, or changed the dose — all in reverse chronological order.

You can also add protocols with a future start date to plan what you want to try next. This is useful for keeping a running list of things you want to experiment with.

Interventions

These are the things you reach for when you're in the middle of a bad episode — medications, techniques, or remedies. When you log an episode, you can record multiple interventions and rate how effective each one was independently.

Running Experiments

This is the core of Baseline. An experiment lets you test one protocol change in a structured way.

Starting an experiment

Go to the Experiments section and tap Start Experiment. Pick the protocol you want to test from the dropdown — or select + Add new protocol to create one right there without leaving the form. When you pick or create a protocol, Baseline suggests a name for the experiment based on the protocol name, but you can edit it to whatever you like.

You can also start an experiment the other way around: when you add a new preventative protocol from the Protocols section, Baseline will ask if you want to track it as an experiment.

For each experiment you define which protocol you're testing, your hypothesis, and how long you'll give it before assessing (default is 3 weeks).

During an experiment

Keep logging as normal. The Experiments page shows your progress and how many weeks remain.

Try not to add other new preventative protocols while an experiment is active. If you change too many things at once, you won't be able to tell what's working.

Assessing the outcome

When your experiment period ends, Baseline will prompt you to assess the outcome. The assessment page shows your actual data — episode frequency before and during, symptom score changes, and a trend chart with the experiment start date marked. You'll rate how well it worked, add notes, and decide whether to continue, pause, or stop the protocol.

Over time, your completed experiments build up an evidence base — a real record of what you've tried and what the data showed.

Your Dashboard

The Dashboard is where your logging pays off. The more consistently you track, the more meaningful the data becomes. Charts and insights appear once you have at least two weeks of episodes logged.

Tracking cards

At the top of the dashboard, you'll see a card for each thing you're tracking, showing your average score this month. Each card also shows the percentage change from your starting baseline — so you can see at a glance if things are improving, worsening, or holding steady.

Episode frequency chart

The bar chart shows how many episodes you've had each week across the last 12 weeks. Vertical lines mark when you started each preventative protocol. This makes it easy to see whether your episode pattern changed after starting a new treatment.

Trend chart

The line chart tracks how each score has moved week by week over the same 12-week period. Protocol start dates are marked here too, so you can visually connect treatment changes with trends.

Protocol impact markers

The vertical lines on both charts mark the start date of each preventative protocol. These matter because they let you visually answer the most important question: did things get better after I started this? Look for changes in the pattern after each marker.

Intervention effectiveness table

Below the charts, the intervention table summarises each intervention you've used: how many times, your average effectiveness rating, and average time to relief. This helps you decide which intervention to reach for first when you need it.

When will my charts appear?

The dashboard shows an empty state message until you have at least two weeks of data. This is intentional — the charts need enough data points to be meaningful. Keep logging consistently and the insights will build up.

Managing What You Track

You set up what you're tracking during onboarding, but you can always change it. Go to What I Track to add new items, edit existing ones, or pause items you no longer want to track.

Two types of tracked items. When you add a new item you choose how to log it:

  • Scale (1–10) — a slider for severity or intensity. Best for things that come in degrees, like pain or fatigue. Your starting baseline and the dashboard's "vs baseline" change are based on this scale.
  • Yes / No — one tap. Best for things that either happen or they don't on a given day, like "bowel movement today," "hair shedding," or "brain fog present." Yes/No items don't have a 1–10 baseline. On the dashboard, they show as a row of dots (green = Yes, red = No, outline = not recorded for that episode) so you can see patterns at a glance.

The type is locked once you've logged at least one entry for that item — that keeps your historical data consistent. If you picked the wrong type at first, you can pause the original item and add a fresh one with the right type. Pausing removes an item from your episode logging form but preserves all your historical data; you can resume it at any time.

Your Account

Signing up. Baseline is free and open to anyone. Create an account with your email and a password, then click the verification link we send to your inbox. The link expires after 24 hours — you can request a fresh one any time from the login page. Your account isn't active until you verify.

From Settings you can:

  • Change your email address or password (both require your current password to confirm)
  • Enable or disable AI logging
  • Delete your account and all associated data

You can log out at any time from the sidebar. Check "Remember me" on the login page to stay logged in for 30 days.

Install as an App

Baseline can be installed directly from your browser — no App Store required. Once installed it opens like a native app, without browser toolbars.

  • iOS: Open Baseline in Safari, tap the Share button, then tap Add to Home Screen
  • Android: Open Baseline in Chrome, tap the browser menu, then tap Add to Home Screen or Install app
  • Desktop: In Chrome or Edge, look for the install icon in the address bar, or check the browser menu for Install Baseline

You may also see an "Install Baseline" prompt at the bottom of the screen when you first visit — just tap it to install.
